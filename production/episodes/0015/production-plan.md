## Intended outcome

The viewer should understand the progression from architectural knowledge, through evaluation and derived findings, to accountable human judgement. Architectural Assurance evaluates what knowledge implies; it does not store another version of architectural truth.

## Editorial treatment

The recorded presenter remains the continuity device. Existing Episode 15 architectural diagrams and established Focus Canvas treatments progressively connect facts, principles, temporal intent and trade-offs. Findings remain visibly separate from accepted knowledge and always pass through human judgement.

## Review gates

- Source and narration: approved inputs
- Scene plan: aligned to the recorded delivery
- Diagrams: existing deterministic SVGs with semantic D2 or PlantUML sources
- Rough cut: continuous playback and contact-sheet review required
- Publication assets: video, thumbnail and subtitles require deterministic validation

## Risks and mitigations

- Findings may appear absolute: use concern, context and judgement language rather than pass/fail treatment.
- Dense conceptual material: keep each scene focused on one architectural question.
- Presenter discontinuity: preserve one continuous source clock and embedded audio.
- Warning fatigue: make the signal-over-noise reduction explicit.
- Closing may feel like a recap list: reconstruct one accumulated architecture instead of presenting episode titles.

No ADR is required. This episode applies the accepted media architecture without changing the renderer.
